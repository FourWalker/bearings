Plotter

A pen that draws Tack. The dot from the Plotter Tide background lifts off, travels, and draws a small vintage line drawing for whatever is going on. The default drawing is a Renaissance scholar reading his notes. Every change of state is the pen redrawing him into something else. The pen is the transition.

Decided (2026-11-24): Tack lives on the board. The direction is A, the Roamer, or a mix of A and B: a home at the top of the panel, narrating where things stand, that roams to a note when it talks about it. Recorded in docs/board/HANDOFF.md. Not started. The board mock below uses the A + B mix, since that is where a pen fits best.

1 · Watch the pen

Click the sheet to make him look up. Type in the box and he listens. Press Enter to send it: he thinks, and if your message mentions a task he writes one. With nothing happening for 25 seconds, he falls asleep.

2 · The drawings

One drawing per mood, in the spirit of a Renaissance notebook: few strokes, brown-black ink, a rust accent. Click a card to have the pen draw it on the sheet above.

3 · How a transformation plays

A transformation happens only when the mood changes to a different drawing. Moods that share a drawing, like reading and looking up, just change pose.

  • Constant pen speed. Each stroke takes time in proportion to its length, so long hair strands take longer than a pupil. In the app the speed is set so the whole transformation fits in about 0.9 s, however many strokes the drawing has.
  • Interruptible. A new mood mid-drawing stops the pen where it is, chases off what's on paper, and starts the new drawing. Nothing waits in a queue.
  • Same drawing, no pen. Typing again while he is already listening, or a second task while he is writing, never redraws.
  • The pen rests on paper. When a drawing is done, the dot vanishes. It only appears while something is being drawn.

4 · Plotter on the board

The A + B mix, with Plotter as the skin. Tack's home is at the top of the panel, reading the board. When he has something to say about a note, the ink at home is chased off, the pen hops across the board on a dotted path, and draws him next to the note. His speech bubble is outlined by the same pen before the words appear. A new task is outlined by the pen first, and then the real note drops in.

What changes in the code, when it's built

  • One more kind of skin. Today every skin is a pixel strip. A skin would name its renderer, sprite or plotter. The moods, the clip rules (tackMood, clipOnMoodChange, clipAfter) and the look menu stay as they are. Only the drawing component differs.
  • Drawings are data. Each drawing is an ordered list of SVG paths, a few hundred bytes, plus a little pose data for its motion (pupil positions, gear angles, wing frames). No images to ship.
  • Timing stays in CSS, as the pixel avatar does. Each path gets pathLength="1" and a stroke-dashoffset animation whose delay is the running sum of the stroke lengths before it. The pen dot rides an offset-path per stroke. No JavaScript animation loop. This page uses one only so the sliders can retime the drawing live.
  • Cheap when quiet. Nothing repaints once a drawing is finished, except its own slow motion (2 to 6 frames a second). Reading holds still after a minute, the candle smoke after 30 s, and everything pauses off screen. Ink wobble is baked into the paths, not a live SVG filter.
  • Colors follow the theme. Under Plotter Tide it uses the theme's own ink and rust, so Tack and the background scene are drawn by the same hand. Under other themes it uses the board's foreground and accent colors.
  • Mobile. Same path data, drawn with react-native-svg. The first cut can use Still mode on mobile (instant swaps), and add the pen later.
